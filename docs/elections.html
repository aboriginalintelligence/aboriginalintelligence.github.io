Part III. Council

No. 05

Election Code

Code

Cite this instrument as No. 05, Election Code.

Summary

Five seats: one chief and four councillors. One councillor must live off reserve. Every elector votes for every seat. Removal takes signatures from 40 percent of electors, then a yes from more than half of the ballots.

The election rule already in force stays in force until members replace it in the way that rule requires.

Part 1. Purpose and authority

1.1 This code sets the election of the chief and councillors. It is a custom election code under Indian Act subsection 2(1), definition of council of the band, paragraph (d).

1.2 It does not apply if Indian Act section 74 applies, or if the First Nation is a participating First Nation under the First Nations Elections Act. For a participating First Nation, section 6 of that Act requires subsequent elections within the 30 days before the term expires, and subsection 28(1) sets a four-year term. Those sections, not this code, would govern.

1.3 The election rule already in force stays in force until the electors replace it in the way that rule requires.

Part 2. Voters, candidates, and the day

2.1 Every elector votes for all five seats. Living on reserve or off reserve does not take away the vote.

2.2 A candidate must be an elector. A candidate must not stand for more than one position in the same election. A candidate who is immediate family of the electoral officer is not eligible. The electoral officer must reject that nomination in writing within 2 days.

2.3 A person who is serving a sentence of imprisonment on nomination day is not eligible. A chief or councillor who, while in office, is sentenced to imprisonment for more than 30 consecutive days ceases to hold office on the day of sentence. This follows the idea in First Nations Elections Act paragraph 28(2)(a), which applies by its own words only to a participating First Nation. It is not a lifetime ban. A lifetime ban would be open to a Charter challenge, and this code does not use one.

2.4 The term and the fixed election day are constitution sections 4.6, 4.7, and 4.8. Council must not change them.

2.5 The five seats are one chief and four councillors. One councillor must live off reserve. Members can change the number only by the amendment vote, at least 120 days before election day. The change applies to the next election. It does not remove the people now in office.

2.6 A person who runs for the off-reserve seat writes on the nomination paper where they live. If that line says they live on reserve, the electoral officer rejects the nomination in writing within 2 days.

2.7 Any elector may challenge that address in writing within 5 days after the candidate list is posted. The electoral officer decides in writing within 5 days. The appeal body decides an appeal within 7 days. If the person does not live off reserve, votes for that person are not counted. If that decision comes after the count, the seat is empty and section 2.8 applies.

2.8 The electoral officer posts an empty seat within 2 days of learning it is empty. If more than 180 days remain before the next election day, the officer holds a by-election for that seat within 45 days, on 21 days' notice. The off-reserve seat keeps the rule in section 2.6. If 180 days or fewer remain, the seat stays empty until election day. Council must not pick a replacement. A council appointment is void.

Part 3. Electoral officer

3.1 The electors appoint the electoral officer by a majority of votes cast, at least 90 days before election day. The officer is not a member of council, not a candidate, not an employee who reports to council, and not immediate family of a candidate.

3.2 Council must not appoint the electoral officer. An appointment signed by council is void. If the electors have not appointed an officer by day 90, the appeal body must appoint one within 14 days and post the name the same day.

3.3 The officer controls the ballot, the voters list, and the count. Council must give the officer the Band List, a room, and a budget line within 7 days of appointment. If council does not, the officer must post that fact on day 8, and council honoraria stop on day 8 until the list and the funds are provided.

3.4 The officer must post a notice of election 60 days before election day. The notice must state the positions, the fixed day, how to get a mail ballot, and the nomination deadline.

Part 4. Nominations, ballots, and the count

4.1 Nominations close 30 days before election day, at noon, local time. A nomination needs two electors other than the candidate, and the candidate's written consent, filed with the officer by that hour. A late nomination is void.

4.2 The officer must post the candidate list within 2 days after nominations close.

4.3 Voting is by secret ballot. A mail ballot must be available to every elector who asks in writing by 14 days before election day. The officer must mail or otherwise send it within 3 days of the request. An elector may vote once for each position. A second ballot for the same position is rejected.

4.4 No person may offer money, goods, work, or any other valuable consideration to influence a vote. No elector may accept it. This code does not decide a criminal charge. A criminal investigation belongs to the police and the Crown. If the appeal body finds, on written reasons, that a candidate did this, the candidate is disqualified and must not be nominated for 5 years. The body must decide within 21 days of a complaint filed within 14 days of election day.

4.5 The officer must post the count within 1 day of the close of the poll. A tie is broken by a draw conducted by the officer in front of two electors who are not candidates, the same day, and the method must be written down.

4.6 A candidate may ask the officer for a recount in writing within 5 days of the posting. The officer must recount within 5 days of the request and post the result the same day.

Part 5. Disputes

5.1 An elector may contest the election by a written complaint to the appeal body within 14 days of the posted count, on the ground that a breach of this code is likely to have changed the result.

5.2 The appeal body must decide in writing within 21 days. It may confirm the result or set the election aside and order a new election within 45 days. It must not install a candidate who did not receive the highest count.

5.3 This internal contest does not remove the right to go to court. Where the First Nations Elections Act applies, sections 30 to 35 of that Act are the contest path: an elector applies to the Federal Court or the superior court of the province, within 30 days after the results are announced, on the ground that a contravention is likely to have affected the result. This Part cannot replace those sections.

Part 6. Removal by the electors

6.1 Electors can remove the chief or a councillor. They file a petition with the appeal body. The petition names the person, the seat, the rule, the date, and what happened. It needs original signatures from at least 40 percent of the electors.

6.2 The appeal body checks the names within 14 days and posts the number that count. A person who is not an elector does not count. The same person does not count twice. If the number is under 40 percent, the petition fails.

6.3 If the number is 40 percent or more, the electoral officer holds a vote. Every elector gets 30 days' notice and a mail ballot. Council cannot cancel the vote, move the date, or cut off the money for it. A resolution that tries to do that is void.

6.4 The person named may file a written reply within 14 days. The electoral officer posts it within 2 days. The ballot says yes or no. If more than half of the ballots say yes, the person leaves office at noon the next day. The empty seat is filled under section 2.8.

6.5 The public notice states the rule and the signature count. It does not say the person committed a crime. This vote is not a criminal case, and it does not replace a court.

6.6 The 40 percent figure is a draft number. The vote itself has no second minimum.

Part 7. Amendment

7.1 Council must not amend this election code by resolution. A resolution that purports to amend it is void on the day it is signed. The appeal body must post that fact within 14 days.

7.2 An amendment passes only if a majority of the votes cast are yes and at least 25 percent of the electors cast a ballot.

7.3 The electoral officer must post the full proposed text at least 30 days before the vote. A vote held on shorter notice is void.

7.4 An amendment that removes or weakens a spending limit, a signature rule, a ban on voting on one's own pay, honorarium, travel, or contract, publication of financial statements or the remuneration schedule, a term limit, the fixed election date, the conflict register, a recusal rule, a right-to-information deadline, the whistleblower channel to the appeal body, the independence of the appeal body, the off-reserve councillor position, the rule that one person must not commit more than $10,000, the open invitation for a contract over $25,000, the public mandate and the quarterly report, the removal petition, or the rule that members, not council alone, amend a law does not pass under 7.2. It passes only if a majority of all electors vote yes. If that count is not met, the amendment is void.

7.5 The electoral officer must post the count within 3 days.

Part 8. Review and coming into force

8.1 The appeal body must review this election code every 4 years, counted from the day it comes into force, and must post a written report within 60 days after that anniversary. The report does not amend this election code.

8.2 Within 90 days of that report, council must either put a recommended amendment to the electors under Part 7, or post written reasons for not doing so. If council does neither, the appeal body must post that omission on day 91, and council honoraria stop on day 91 until one of those two things is done.

8.3 this election code comes into force on the day after the electors approve it under Part 7. Until that day it is a draft. It does not repeal an election code, by-law, or policy already in force.

8.4 Where a law already in force sets its own replacement rule, this election code replaces that law only if the electors also meet that rule.

8.5 The records officer must keep the adopted text posted for at least 10 years. If it is not posted by day 10 after the vote, the records officer must post a written reason that day, and the appeal body must appoint a replacement records officer within a further 10 days.
